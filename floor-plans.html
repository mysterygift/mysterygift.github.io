FLOOR PLANS

FLOOR PLANS

Plan every setup before you get there.

Draw a set or a unit base to scale. Plot the cameras, cast, lights and grip for each shot. Work out the power, the truss and the DMX, check the kit against Equipment, then print it.

Experimental. This is still being tested on set. Switch it on in Settings, under Developer, with Show experimental features. It may change between releases, and it stores its data on your computer only.

The short version

  • Drawn to scale, over a map or your own picture.
  • Cameras, cast, lights and grip for every shot.
  • Power, cables, truss and DMX worked out.
  • Kit checked against Equipment, then printed.
01

Draw it to scale

Draw over a map of the location, to scale and turned to match north, or over your own picture. Set the scale from a plan width or by measuring something you know. Snap to a grid of any size, keep lines at 90 degrees or draw freely, and measure anything with the ruler. Select, lock, copy and paste groups. Duplicate a plan for a night version, or save part of one as a template for every production.

02

Plot every setup

Pick the scene and shot along the bottom. Place cameras, lettered A, B, C with a colour each, and a wedge showing what the lens sees. Place cast in their Bookings colours. Add lights and grip. Show a move with an arrow, see the distance from each camera to each person, and see the last setup faintly underneath. The shot description stays in view as you work.

03

About 160 items, to real size

Lights from tungsten to HMI to LED, camera support, grip, unit base and household furniture, all drawn at real size. Makers' ranges from ARRI, Aputure, amaran, Nanlite, Nanlux and Godox come with their power, weight and colour range, and take modifiers: softboxes, lanterns, domes, lenses, grids and barn doors.

04

Colour and stands

Set a light's colour temperature and the beam is tinted to match, with the white balance shown under its name on the plan and in the PDF. Add a gel as a note. New lights come on a suitable stand, which you can change.

05

Power and cables

Mark the location's power, from 13A to 125A industrial to 400A Powerlock, at 230 V, 400 V or 110 V, and the distribution plugged into it. Add generators and battery power stations with a runtime estimate. Draw cables from a supply to what it feeds, and see the voltage drop, using the BS 7671 method. Work abroad with the local mains. A pick list gives the cables in hire lengths and the adaptors they need. These are planning figures, not a design: temporary power is designed and tested by a competent person.

06

Truss and rigging

Plan 290, 400 and 520 box truss, with the connectors worked out where it meets. Add the venue's rigging points with their safe working load and height, and chain hoists that lift the truss. Hang lights on it and see what each hoist lifts, with warnings when a hoist is rated over its point or short of chain.

07

Patch the lights

Patch every light in a DMX view, with clashes in red. Let Albatross patch the unpatched ones. Export the patch as a PDF sheet for the desk, a CSV list, or an MVR file that grandMA3, Vectorworks and Capture can open.

08

Kit list against Equipment

Pick a shoot day and see every piece of kit the plans need next to what is on order in Equipment: on order, short, or not on order. Add the shortfall to Equipment as planned hires, or make the day's kit list in one click. Cables and adaptors are checked too, and the list shows the weight of the kit on order.

09

See the sun

Set the plan's location, pick a day and slide the time. The sun's path runs round the plan, and a dashed ray shows where the light comes from.

10

Safety, for the risk assessment

Mark fire exits, extinguishers, first aid points, an assembly point and keep-out zones. The risk assessment suggests hazards for the kit you placed, and its PDF ends with the site plans showing the way out and where to gather.

11

Print what you need

Export a shoot day, a location, a scene or chosen shots, one setup to a landscape page. Choose what is on it: cameras and cast, lighting and power, other kit. Or print a gaffer's lighting plot on A3 or A4, drawn at a standard scale with unit numbers, DMX addresses and every cable run.

Connected

Locations supply the maps and addresses. Cast take their colours from Bookings. Kit is checked against Equipment, and the shoot day's plans go into the risk assessment and the day pack.
