01

Start from a template

Create a new project and pick Default or Blank. Default gives you the chart of accounts above, Above the Line and Below the Line totals already set up, a 10% contingency rule, starter tasks and a small set of deliverables. Blank gives you nothing and you build your own.

02

Your chart, your way

Add accounts, rename them, give each a colour and put them in your order. Archive an account to stop new spend without touching your history. Delete one that was never used. Money posts to the lowest level accounts only, and the headers above them roll everything up.

03

Reports your way

See the cost report as the full chart of accounts, or by groups you define. Set your own production totals, such as Above the Line and Below the Line subtotals. Print it, export it as a PDF, or export the line items to CSV.

04

Fringes and contingency as rules

Set fringes and contingency as percentage rules and choose which accounts each one applies to. They sit on top of your budget, separate from actual spend, so your totals stay honest.

05

Budget revisions

Keep more than one version of your budget. Start a revision blank, or as a copy of an existing one. One revision is live at a time and switching takes a confirmation. Compare revisions in a read-only summary.

06

Vendors, POs and invoices

A vendor has purchase orders and invoices, and every spend can point at them. Raise or lower a PO at any time and each change is logged with a reason. Invoices run from draft to paid, and a due date creates a task that completes when you mark it paid.

07

Match spend to budget lines

Match Expenses links spend to budget line items, in whole or in part. Anything left unmatched stands out, so you can reconcile before wrap.

08

Tax credits, VAT and currency

Switch on tax credits per production and tag the spend that qualifies. Track VAT on each expense and set the reclaim rate by transaction type. Budget in your production currency, set display conversion in Settings, and lock the exchange rate on a PO in another currency.

How it fits together

The money trail.

01

Vendor

A company you pay. Keep it in one project, or share it across all of them.

→
02

Purchase order

Agreed spend with a vendor. Raise or lower it any time. Every change is logged with a reason.

→
03

Invoice

What the vendor bills you. Draft, received, approved, paid or overdue. Link it to a PO.

→
04

Spend

What you actually pay. Links to one or more POs, with an amount on each, and to an invoice or a receipt.

→
05

Budget account

Every spend posts to an account, so your actuals move the moment you log it.

  • Vendor|has|purchase orders and invoices
  • Invoice|can link to|a purchase order
  • Spend|links to|one or more POs, with an amount on each
  • Spend|is backed by|an invoice or a receipt. A receipt needs no vendor
  • Spend|posts to|one budget account

PO and invoice matching applies to Purchase, Rental and Deposit spend. Albatross warns you when spend goes over a PO's balance, when amounts do not add up, or when an invoice sits on a different PO. It never stops you saving. Use Increase PO to cover to raise the PO and log the amendment on the spot.

Connected

Spend posts to accounts, accounts roll up to your totals, and invoice due dates become tasks. Wrap checks that it is all reconciled.
